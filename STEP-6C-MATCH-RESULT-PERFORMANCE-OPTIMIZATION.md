# STEP 6C - MATCH-RESULT PERFORMANCE OPTIMIZATION

## Scope

This step implements only the measured `applyAiFixtureResults()` / `RECORD_MATCH_RESULT` hot-path optimization identified by Step 6B.

The following were not changed:

- match-engine rules or score calculation
- RNG or seeds
- fixture generation or fixture counts
- season progression rules
- public APIs
- player, manager, event, memory, consequence, or fixture semantics
- batching that would change sequential reducer behavior

## Baseline

Step 6B identified `RECORD_MATCH_RESULT` as the dominant full-world cost. The canonical live world used for profiling contains:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Clubs | 1,737 |
| Players | 41,521 |
| Leagues | 81 |
| Scheduled fixtures | 35,717 |

The baseline report measured result application at approximately 36 ms per fixture in bounded samples. The prior pre-change benchmark notes recorded approximately:

| Sample | Total apply time | Per fixture |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 2,689 ms | 38.98 ms |
| 1,000 | 2,801 ms | 40.60 ms |
| 5,000 | 2,718 ms | 39.40 ms |

These historical samples are retained as the comparison record from the preceding session. They were not generated by a committed benchmark script, so they should be treated as indicative rather than statistically rigorous.

## Change implemented

The `RECORD_MATCH_RESULT` reducer now performs one fixture-ID index lookup and reuses the resulting fixture record during the immutable update.

Previously, the path located the fixture and then performed another lookup while rebuilding the fixture array. The updated path:

1. calls `findIndex()` once;
2. reuses the indexed fixture for the idempotency check and score update;
3. copies the fixture array once with `slice()`;
4. replaces only the indexed fixture entry;
5. leaves all existing player updates, match/event appends, club-memory writes, consequences, confidence updates, and lock-clearing behavior unchanged.

This is a local allocation and lookup reduction, not a redesign of state ownership or reducer sequencing.

### Interpretation

The result-application path remains the measured bottleneck. The post-change measurements fall in the same broad range as the baseline, with normal variance from a single-process benchmark and state size. The 1,000-fixture sample was lower than the recorded baseline, the 100-fixture sample was higher, and the 5,000-fixture sample was slightly lower. There is not enough repeated sampling to claim a statistically reliable percentage improvement.

The safe conclusion is therefore:

- semantic correctness was preserved;
- duplicate fixture scanning was removed;
- the reducer remains approximately a 30-42 ms-per-result operation at this workload;
- the optimization is low risk but insufficient by itself to make a 35,717-fixture season cheap.

## Determinism and invariants

No score-generation code, seed handling, or result construction changed. The reducer still receives the same `RECORD_MATCH_RESULT` action shape and applies results sequentially through `applyAiFixtureResults()`.

The focused integration suite covers the relevant state invariants, including:

- replaying an identical played fixture is a no-op;
- invalid fixture IDs do not crash the reducer;
- scheduled fixtures become played exactly once;
- batch application matches sequential application;
- match and event retention remains consistent.

## Remaining bottlenecks

The measured cost that remains is the work intentionally left untouched for semantic safety:

- per-player morale, form, reputation, market value, and rating-history updates;
- immutable player dictionary copying;
- match and event array appends;
- two club-memory writes per result;
- consequence processing;
- manager confidence and pending-fixture handling;
- repeated fixture/state scans around the season simulation loop.

A larger improvement would require changing one or more of these behaviors or introducing a carefully specified batch boundary. That is outside this constrained Step 6C change and should not be started without a separate semantic equivalence design and benchmark plan.

## Final status

Step 6C is complete as a narrow, behavior-preserving optimization.

The change reduces redundant fixture lookup work and passes the focused correctness and type checks. It does not eliminate the full-world bottleneck, and the available single-run measurements do not support claiming a major speedup. Step 6D has not been started.
